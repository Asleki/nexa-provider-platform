1. Purpose

The Issuance Authority Matrix defines who is authorized to issue, approve,
modify, suspend, revoke, retire, and oversee identifiers within the Master
Registry Foundation.

Its purpose is to ensure that every identifier is created under controlled
governance, preventing unauthorized issuance, duplication, fraud, and
inconsistent registry management across the Nexa Provider Platform.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

2. Governance Principles

The Registry Foundation follows these principles:

• Every identifier has a clearly defined issuing authority.
• Every issuing authority has a defined scope.
• No authority may issue identifiers outside its approved scope.
• Issuance actions must be auditable.
• Issuance responsibilities may be delegated only through approved governance.
• Every issued identifier must have a traceable issuing authority.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

3. Authority Levels

Level 1 — Platform Authority

Responsible for defining registry policies, identifier standards, numbering
strategies, and governance rules.

Typical Authority:

• Platform Governance Board
• Registry Governance Committee

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Level 2 — Registry Authority

Responsible for administering an entire registry.

Responsibilities include:

• Registry configuration
• Issuance approval
• Registry maintenance
• Registry retirement
• Registry audits

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Level 3 — Operational Authority

Responsible for issuing identifiers during normal business operations.

Examples:

• Customer Registration Officer
• Supplier Registration Officer
• Employee Administrator
• Manufacturer Registrar

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Level 4 — Verification Authority

Responsible for validating supporting information before issuance.

Examples:

• Identity Verification Officer
• Compliance Officer
• Quality Assurance Officer

5. Authority Responsibilities

Platform Governance

Responsible for:

• registry standards;
• numbering strategies;
• governance policies;
• approval frameworks;
• compliance.

Registry Administrator

Responsible for:

• registry management;
• issuance oversight;
• suspension approval;
• retirement approval;
• audit coordination.

Operational Issuer

Responsible for:

• collecting registration information;
• issuing identifiers;
• initiating lifecycle events.

Verification Officer

Responsible for:

• validating submitted information;
• confirming documentation;
• approving verification status.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

6. Delegated Issuance

Issuance authority may be delegated only when:

• formally approved;
• documented;
• time limited where applicable;
• auditable;
• revocable.

Delegated authority shall never exceed the authority of the delegating body.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

7. Approval Requirements

High-risk identifiers require approval before issuance.

Examples include:

• Citizen IDs
• Government-linked identifiers
• Provider identifiers
• High-security system identities

Routine operational identifiers may be issued directly by authorized officers,
subject to verification requirements.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

8. Suspension Authority

Only authorized Registry Administrators or Platform Governance may suspend an
identifier.

Every suspension must record:

• identifier;
• reason;
• authorizing officer;
• timestamp;
• expected review date.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

9. Revocation Authority

Revocation permanently invalidates an identifier.

Only Platform Governance or an authorized Registry Administrator may revoke an
identifier.

Every revocation requires:

• documented justification;
• approval reference;
• audit record;
• lifecycle event.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

10. Retirement Authority

Identifiers reaching the end of their operational lifecycle may be retired.

Retirement does not delete historical records.

Retired identifiers remain available for audit and historical reference.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

11. Emergency Authority

Platform Governance may authorize emergency actions during:

• security incidents;
• fraud investigations;
• legal requirements;
• system compromise;
• regulatory compliance events.

Emergency actions must be reviewed after the incident concludes.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

12. Audit Requirements

Every issuance-related action shall record:

• Event ID
• Registry
• Identifier
• Issuing Authority
• Verifying Authority
• Approving Authority
• Timestamp
• Device
• Location
• Reason
• Approval Reference

These records form part of the permanent audit trail.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

13. Separation of Duties

Where practical, the following responsibilities should be performed by
different authorized individuals:

• Verification
• Issuance
• Approval
• Audit

This reduces the risk of fraud, conflicts of interest, and unauthorized
identifier creation.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

14. Future Compatibility

The authority framework defined in this document applies to all future
registries introduced into the Nexa Provider Platform.

Future registries shall assign issuing authorities consistent with these
governance principles while preserving auditability, accountability, and
registry integrity.
